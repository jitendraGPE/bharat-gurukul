# Topic guides for volunteer presenters and circle leaders

Every topic is presented by a volunteer. This guide gives the presenter and the four circle leaders what they need for each month: the message, verses with sources, what to cover, circle activities, games, a quiz with answers, the life question from the epics, and the month's sankalp.

**Every gathering opens** with invited delegates lighting the lamp with a child and an elder, and the invocation *Oṁ saha nāvavatu, saha nau bhunaktu, saha vīryaṁ karavāvahai, tejasvi nāv adhītam astu, mā vidviṣāvahai* (may we learn together, grow together, and never be hostile to one another).

**Rules for every presenter:** recite each verse, give its source and explain it simply; keep the talk to 40 minutes; lead one practice everyone does together; speak to all ages; never overstate a historical claim, and say when something is tradition rather than history.

Ramayana references follow Valmiki (Gita Press sarga numbers). Mahabharata references follow the BORI critical edition (CE chapter.verse).

## 01 · January: The Vedas

**The roots of Bharat** · 2027-01-17 · Makar Sankranti

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** The Vedas are India’s oldest texts. They teach that truth is one, that we must live in harmony with nature, and that people must walk and decide together.

> Tradition holds the Vedas to be eternal (apauruṣeya); most scholars date the Rigveda to about 1500–1200 BCE.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Tat savitur vareṇyaṁ bhargo devasya dhīmahi, dhiyo yo naḥ pracodayāt* | Rigveda 3.62.10 (Gayatri) | We meditate on the radiant light of the divine sun; may it inspire our minds. |
| *Ekaṁ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti* | Rigveda 1.164.46 | Truth is one; the wise call it by many names. |
| *Saṁ gacchadhvaṁ saṁ vadadhvaṁ saṁ vo manāṁsi jānatām* | Rigveda 10.191.2 | Walk together, speak together, let your minds be in harmony. |
| *Mātā bhūmiḥ putro ahaṁ pṛthivyāḥ* | Atharvaveda 12.1.12 | The earth is my mother; I am her child. |

**What to cover**

- The four Vedas: Rig (hymns), Sama (melodies, the root of Indian music), Yajur (ritual) and Atharva (daily life and healing).
- Women seers such as Ghosha, Apala and Lopamudra composed Vedic hymns.
- The Vedas were kept alive by memory for over 3,000 years; UNESCO recognises Vedic chanting as world heritage.

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Bal Gurukul | Learn the Gayatri with a clapping rhythm; Surya Namaskar |
| Yuva Sabha | A memory game in the Vedic krama style |
| Karma Sabha | A meeting where everyone speaks before anyone decides |
| Vriddha Sabha | Prayers we learnt by heart as children |

**Games**

- **Mantra relay:** In pairs, pass a line of the Gayatri word by word, forwards and then backwards, the way the Vedas were memorised (krama style).
- **Sun salutation freeze:** Children flow through Surya Namaskar; when the leader rings a bell, everyone freezes in the current pose.

**Quiz**

1. How many Vedas are there? (A) Two (B) Three (C) Four (D) Six
2. Which Veda is called the root of Indian music? (A) Rigveda (B) Samaveda (C) Yajurveda (D) Atharvaveda
3. “Ekaṁ sad viprā bahudhā vadanti” means… (A) Truth is one; the wise call it by many names (B) Walk together, speak together (C) The earth is my mother (D) Arise and awake
4. The Gayatri mantra is a prayer to… (A) Savitr, the sun (B) Agni, fire (C) Indra (D) Varuna
5. Which woman seer composed Vedic hymns? (A) Ghosha (B) Draupadi (C) Kunti (D) Mandodari

Answers: 1 C, 2 B, 3 A, 4 A, 5 A

**Life's question from the epics:** When plans collapse. Told he is to be exiled instead of crowned, Rama stays calm and still bows to his father and to Kaikeyi. (Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda 18)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** Greet the rising sun each morning and sit in silence for three minutes.

## 02 · February: Vedic sciences

**Knowledge born from the Vedas** · 2027-02-21 · Vasant Panchami

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** To understand the Vedas, our ancestors built sciences of sound, grammar, mathematics, astronomy, health and building. Learning and devotion were never separate.

> The popular “Vedic Maths” speed tricks come from a 20th-century book by Swami Bharati Krishna Tirtha, not from the Vedas themselves.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Sarasvati namastubhyaṁ varade kāmarūpiṇi, vidyārambhaṁ kariṣyāmi siddhir bhavatu me sadā* | Traditional prayer before study | Salutations to Saraswati; as I begin to learn, may I always succeed. |
| *Yathā śikhā mayūrāṇāṁ nāgānāṁ maṇayo yathā, tadvad vedāṅga-śāstrāṇāṁ gaṇitaṁ mūrdhani sthitam* | Vedanga Jyotisha | As the crest on the peacock and the jewel on the cobra, so mathematics stands at the head of the sciences. |
| *Samadoṣaḥ samāgniśca samadhātu malakriyaḥ, prasannātmendriyamanaḥ svastha ityabhidhīyate* | Sushruta Samhita, Sutrasthana | Health is balance of body and digestion, with a happy self, senses and mind. |

**What to cover**

- The six Vedangas: phonetics, grammar, metre, meaning of words, astronomy and ritual.
- The Shulba Sutras give rope-and-peg geometry for building altars, including the relationship now called Pythagoras’ theorem.
- Ayurveda teaches health as balance: daily routine (dinacharya), food and sleep.

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Bal Gurukul | Rope geometry and a Saraswati vandana |
| Yuva Sabha | Mental-maths games and a science quiz |
| Karma Sabha | Design a realistic daily routine for working life |
| Vriddha Sabha | Household knowledge of food and seasons |

**Games**

- **Rope geometry challenge:** Teams get a rope knotted into 12 equal parts and race to make a right angle with sides of 3, 4 and 5 parts.
- **Vedanga match:** Match six cards (phonetics, grammar, metre, meaning, astronomy, ritual) to their Sanskrit names.

**Quiz**

1. The Shulba Sutras give rules for building… (A) Fire altars (B) Ships (C) Roads (D) Forts
2. How many Vedangas are there? (A) Four (B) Six (C) Eight (D) Twelve
3. Which Upaveda is about health? (A) Ayurveda (B) Dhanurveda (C) Gandharvaveda (D) Sthapatyaveda
4. A rope triangle with sides 3, 4 and 5 gives… (A) A right angle (B) A circle (C) A square (D) A straight line
5. In Ayurveda, the daily routine is called… (A) Dinacharya (B) Ritucharya (C) Sankalp (D) Yatra

Answers: 1 A, 2 B, 3 A, 4 A, 5 A

**Life's question from the epics:** Laziness and putting things off. Vidura names six faults that hold a person back: too much sleep, drowsiness, fear, anger, laziness and delay. (Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva (Vidura Niti), CE 5.33.66)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** Ten minutes of new learning every day, and one fixed time to sleep and wake.

## 03 · March: Upanishads

**Knowing yourself** · 2027-03-21 · Holi

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** The Upanishads turn the search inward. They ask “Who am I?” and answer that the same divine self lives in every being.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Asato mā sad gamaya, tamaso mā jyotir gamaya, mṛtyor mā amṛtaṁ gamaya* | Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 1.3.28 | Lead me from untruth to truth, from darkness to light, from death to immortality. |
| *Tat tvam asi* | Chandogya Upanishad 6.8.7 | You are that. |
| *Mātṛdevo bhava, pitṛdevo bhava, ācāryadevo bhava, atithidevo bhava* | Taittiriya Upanishad 1.11.2 | Treat mother, father, teacher and guest as divine. |
| *Satyam eva jayate* | Mundaka Upanishad 3.1.6 | Truth alone triumphs. (India’s national motto.) |

**What to cover**

- Shvetaketu and the salt: unseen in water, yet present in every sip, like the self in all things (Chandogya 6.13).
- Satyakama Jabala tells his teacher the plain truth about his birth and is accepted for his honesty (Chandogya 4.4).
- At the end of study, the teacher says: “Speak the truth, follow dharma.” (Taittiriya 1.11.1)

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Bal Gurukul | The salt-in-water experiment |
| Yuva Sabha | Write “I am…” ten times, then cross out every label |
| Karma Sabha | Honesty at work when it costs you |
| Vriddha Sabha | How atithi devo bhava was lived at home |

**Games**

- **The salt mystery:** Dissolve salt in water in front of everyone. Children taste a drop from the top, middle and bottom, then guess the lesson.
- **Beyond the label:** Everyone writes three labels about themselves, then swaps: guess who is behind the labels.

**Quiz**

1. “Tat tvam asi” (you are that) comes from which Upanishad? (A) Chandogya (B) Katha (C) Isha (D) Mundaka
2. India’s national motto “Satyameva jayate” comes from… (A) Mundaka Upanishad (B) Rigveda (C) Bhagavad Gita (D) Ramayana
3. In the salt story, the self is like salt in water because it is… (A) Present everywhere though unseen (B) Only in the heart (C) Only in the sky (D) Only in books
4. Why did the teacher accept Satyakama as a student? (A) He told the plain truth (B) He was rich (C) He was strong (D) He was a prince
5. “Asato mā sad gamaya” asks to be led from… (A) Untruth to truth (B) Village to city (C) Poverty to wealth (D) War to peace

Answers: 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 4 A, 5 A

**Life's question from the epics:** What real wealth is. The Yaksha asks Yudhishthira who is truly rich. The answer: one to whom joy and sorrow, past and future, are alike. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva (Yaksha Prashna), CE 3.297.62–64)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** Five minutes of journalling each night: who am I beyond my roles today?

## 04 · April: Kathopanishad

**Nachiketa and mastering the mind** · 2027-04-18 · Ram Navami

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** A boy, Nachiketa, has the courage to ask Death itself about the meaning of life. His teacher shows that a trained mind, guided by a clear intellect, carries us to our goal.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Ātmānaṁ rathinaṁ viddhi śarīraṁ ratham eva tu, buddhiṁ tu sārathiṁ viddhi manaḥ pragraham eva ca* | Kathopanishad 1.3.3 | The self is the rider, the body the chariot, the intellect the charioteer, the mind the reins. |
| *Anyac chreyo ’nyad utaiva preyaḥ* | Kathopanishad 1.2.1 | The good is one thing, the pleasant another. The wise choose the good. |
| *Uttiṣṭhata jāgrata prāpya varān nibodhata* | Kathopanishad 1.3.14 | Arise, awake, and learn from the wise. (Swami Vivekananda’s call to youth.) |

**What to cover**

- Nachiketa waits three days at Yama’s door and refuses wealth and pleasure, asking only for true knowledge.
- The chariot: untrained horses (the senses) run wild unless the charioteer (intellect) holds the reins (mind).
- Shreyas over preyas: choose what is good in the long run over what is pleasant now.

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Bal Gurukul | Draw and label the chariot; a question box |
| Yuva Sabha | List your “horses” and one rein for each |
| Karma Sabha | Short-term targets versus long-term good |
| Vriddha Sabha | Discipline in our youth, without screens |

**Games**

- **Chariot tug:** Five children are the horses, one the charioteer holding a rope as reins; the charioteer must steer the horses to a goal.
- **Question box race:** Teams write the deepest question they can; the whole group votes for the most Nachiketa-like question.

**Quiz**

1. From whom did Nachiketa learn the secret of life? (A) Yama (B) Vishvamitra (C) Vasishtha (D) Indra
2. In the chariot image, the reins stand for… (A) The mind (B) The senses (C) The intellect (D) The body
3. The horses in the chariot image are… (A) The senses (B) The mind (C) The self (D) The road
4. “Shreyas” means… (A) The good (B) The pleasant (C) Wealth (D) Fame
5. “Uttiṣṭhata jāgrata” became the call to youth of… (A) Swami Vivekananda (B) Rabindranath Tagore (C) Sri Aurobindo (D) Adi Shankaracharya

Answers: 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 4 A, 5 A

**Life's question from the epics:** Anger, pride and greed. The Yaksha teaches: give up pride and you are loved; give up anger and you do not grieve; give up greed and you are happy. (Mahabharata, Vana Parva (Yaksha Prashna), CE 3.297.57)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** Ten screen-free minutes a day, and one family hour a week with all phones in a basket.

## 05 · May: Dhyana and Pranayama

**Calm in every storm** · 2027-05-16 · Buddha Purnima

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** The sages found that when the breath is steady, the mind becomes steady. Breath and meditation are tools anyone can use, at any age.

> Take care: no breath-holding for anyone with high blood pressure, heart conditions or pregnancy. Stop at any dizziness.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Tāṁ yogam iti manyante sthirām indriya-dhāraṇām* | Kathopanishad 2.3.11 | The steady holding of the senses is called yoga: one of the earliest definitions. |
| *Oṁ ity etad akṣaram idaṁ sarvam* | Mandukya Upanishad 1 | Om: this sound is all this. |
| *Cale vāte calaṁ cittaṁ niścale niścalaṁ bhavet* | Hatha Yoga Pradipika 2.2 (a later text recording older practice) | When the breath moves, the mind moves; when the breath is still, the mind is still. |

**What to cover**

- Om chanting: three long Oms.
- Anulom-vilom (alternate-nostril breathing), five slow rounds.
- Bhramari (bee breath): a soft hum with ears closed; it calms children quickly.
- Simple dhyana: watch the breath for two minutes.

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Bal Gurukul | Bee breath and a stillness game |
| Yuva Sabha | A five-minute exam-calm routine |
| Karma Sabha | A three-minute desk meditation |
| Vriddha Sabha | Gentle pranayama on chairs |

**Games**

- **Stillness statue:** Everyone sits still with eyes closed; anyone who moves before the bell quietly stands. The last still person wins.
- **Bee-breath choir:** Groups hum bhramari together; the calmest, longest hum wins a clap.

**Quiz**

1. Bhramari is also called… (A) Bee breath (B) Lion breath (C) Fire breath (D) Cooling breath
2. Anulom-vilom is… (A) Alternate-nostril breathing (B) Holding the breath (C) Fast breathing (D) Humming
3. The Kathopanishad calls the steady holding of the senses… (A) Yoga (B) Tapas (C) Seva (D) Bhakti
4. Who should not practise breath-holding? (A) People with high blood pressure or heart conditions (B) Children who are calm (C) Anyone before breakfast (D) Nobody
5. The Mandukya Upanishad is about… (A) The sound Om (B) The Ramayana (C) The planets (D) Ayurveda

Answers: 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 4 A, 5 A

**Life's question from the epics:** Forgiveness. Vidura tells the king: forgiveness alone is the highest peace (kṣamaikā śāntir uttamā). (Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva (Vidura Niti), CE 5.33.48)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** Five minutes of slow breathing or silent sitting every morning.

## 06 · June: Yoga

**Strong body, steady mind, good conduct** · 2027-06-20 · International Day of Yoga (21 June)

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** Yoga is an eight-step path that begins with how we treat others and ourselves, and ends in a still, clear mind.

> Yoga’s roots are in the Upanishads; Patanjali gathered the teaching into the Yoga Sutras, which scholars date to roughly the 2nd century BCE–4th century CE.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ* | Yoga Sutra 1.2 | Yoga is the stilling of the movements of the mind. |
| *Sthira-sukham āsanam* | Yoga Sutra 2.46 | A posture should be steady and comfortable. |
| *Yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam* | Bhagavad Gita 2.50 | Yoga is skill in action. |

**What to cover**

- Yamas (how we treat others): non-violence, truth, not stealing, self-restraint, not hoarding (Yoga Sutra 2.30).
- Niyamas (how we treat ourselves): cleanliness, contentment, discipline, self-study, surrender (Yoga Sutra 2.32).
- In 2014 the UN declared 21 June the International Day of Yoga, co-sponsored by 177 countries.

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Bal Gurukul | Animal asanas; five golden-rule cards |
| Yuva Sabha | Yoga for posture and screen fatigue |
| Karma Sabha | Desk yoga; the yamas as a code of work |
| Vriddha Sabha | Chair yoga |

**Games**

- **Animal asana freeze:** Call out an animal; everyone makes its asana (tree, cobra, butterfly, cat) and holds it for five breaths.
- **Yama-niyama sort:** Sort ten cards into ‘how we treat others’ (yamas) and ‘how we treat ourselves’ (niyamas).

**Quiz**

1. Yoga Sutra 1.2 defines yoga as… (A) Stilling the movements of the mind (B) Standing on one’s head (C) A kind of exercise (D) Chanting mantras
2. How many limbs does Patanjali’s yoga have? (A) Four (B) Six (C) Eight (D) Ten
3. Ahimsa (non-violence) is one of the… (A) Yamas (B) Niyamas (C) Asanas (D) Pranayamas
4. International Day of Yoga is on… (A) 21 June (B) 26 January (C) 15 August (D) 2 October
5. “Yogaḥ karmasu kauśalam” means… (A) Yoga is skill in action (B) Yoga is rest (C) Yoga is prayer (D) Yoga is breath

Answers: 1 A, 2 C, 3 A, 4 A, 5 A

**Life's question from the epics:** Self-mastery at work. Vidura: the wise are not pulled from their purpose by anger, joy, pride or shame, and stay calm like a deep pool of the Ganga. (Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva (Vidura Niti), CE 5.33.17, 26)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** Fifteen minutes of yoga daily, with three rounds of Surya Namaskar.

## 07 · July: Ramayana

**Character, family and keeping your word** · 2027-07-18 · Guru Purnima

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** Character is built through small choices: keeping your word, respecting elders and serving without expecting reward.

> We follow Valmiki’s Ramayana (Gita Press numbering). Scenes such as the Lakshman Rekha and Shabari tasting the berries come from later retellings and are told as such.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Rāmo vigrahavān dharmaḥ* | Valmiki Ramayana, Aranya Kanda 37.13 | Rama is dharma in human form, said even by his enemy Maricha. |
| *Raghukula rīti sadā chali āī, prāna jāhu baru bachanu na jāī* | Tulsidas, Ramcharitmanas, Ayodhya Kand | The way of Raghu’s line: life may go, but a promise must not. |

**What to cover**

- Rama accepts fourteen years of exile to keep his father’s word, without anger (Ayodhya Kanda 18).
- Bharata refuses the throne and rules as caretaker with Rama’s sandals on it (Ayodhya Kanda 112).
- Sita puts a blade of grass between herself and Ravana and rebukes him (Sundara Kanda 21).
- Rama honours the aged Shabari and her service to her teachers (Aranya Kanda 74).

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Bal Gurukul | Story theatre; paper padukas |
| Yuva Sabha | A promise that clashes with a new opportunity |
| Karma Sabha | Bharata as a model of trusteeship |
| Vriddha Sabha | The Ramayana as our grandparents told it |

**Games**

- **Paduka relay:** Teams carry paper padukas on a tray to a ‘throne’ without dropping them, as Bharata carried Rama’s sandals.
- **Who said it?:** Read a line from the Ramayana; teams guess the speaker: Rama, Sita, Bharata, Hanuman, Maricha or Jatayu.

**Quiz**

1. Who composed the original Ramayana? (A) Valmiki (B) Vyasa (C) Tulsidas (D) Kalidasa
2. What did Bharata place on the throne? (A) Rama’s sandals (B) Rama’s bow (C) A crown (D) A lamp
3. Who said “Rāmo vigrahavān dharmaḥ” (Rama is dharma embodied)? (A) Maricha (B) Hanuman (C) Lakshmana (D) Vibhishana
4. Which of these is NOT in Valmiki’s Ramayana? (A) The Lakshman Rekha (B) Jatayu’s fight (C) Bharata ruling with the sandals (D) Sita’s blade of grass
5. Who, though old, fought Ravana to save Sita? (A) Jatayu (B) Sampati (C) Jambavan (D) Sugriva

Answers: 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 4 A, 5 A

**Life's question from the epics:** Rivalry in the family. Bharata could have had the kingdom. Instead he places Rama’s sandals on the throne and rules only in his name. (Ramayana, Ayodhya Kanda 112)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** One act of service at home every day, done without being asked.

## 08 · August: Mahabharata

**Right action in conflict** · 2027-08-15 · Independence Day · Raksha Bandhan

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** Life is full of grey situations. The Mahabharata shows how to choose rightly when every option has a cost, and what happens when greed and pride go unchecked.

> We cite the BORI critical edition (CE). Some famous lines, such as the Yaksha’s “greatest wonder”, appear only in popular printed editions.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Na tat parasya saṁdadhyāt pratikūlaṁ yad ātmanaḥ* | Anushasana Parva, CE 13.114.8 | Do not do to another what is hateful to yourself; this, in brief, is dharma. |
| *Kṣamaikā śāntir uttamā* | Udyoga Parva (Vidura Niti), CE 5.33.48 | Forgiveness alone is the highest peace. |
| *Yato dharmas tato jayaḥ* | Udyoga Parva, CE 5.39.7 | Where there is dharma, there is victory. |

**What to cover**

- The dice game: Yudhishthira keeps staking as Shakuni taunts him (Sabha Parva, CE 2.58).
- Draupadi challenges the court: “Did he lose himself first, or me?” (CE 2.60.7–8)
- The Yaksha Prashna: wisdom, not force, saves the brothers (Vana Parva, CE 3.297).
- Vidura warns Dhritarashtra again and again; the king listens but does not act (Udyoga Parva).

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |
| --- | --- |
| Bal Gurukul | “What would you do?” picture quiz |
| Yuva Sabha | A mock Yaksha Prashna |
| Karma Sabha | The one person who warned us: listening to Vidura |
| Vriddha Sabha | Elders as Bhishma: settling family disputes |

**Games**

- **Yaksha’s riddles:** A volunteer plays the Yaksha by the lake; teams must answer riddles before they may ‘drink’.
- **Dilemma walk:** Read a dilemma; people walk to the ‘yes’ or ‘no’ side of the hall, then each side gives one reason.

**Quiz**

1. According to tradition, who composed the Mahabharata? (A) Vyasa (B) Valmiki (C) Kalidasa (D) Panini
2. In the Yaksha Prashna, whom does Yudhishthira ask to be revived? (A) Nakula (B) Bhima (C) Arjuna (D) Karna
3. “Kṣamaikā śāntir uttamā” (forgiveness is the highest peace) is the counsel of… (A) Vidura (B) Shakuni (C) Duryodhana (D) Drona
4. Who asked the court, “Did he lose himself first, or me?” (A) Draupadi (B) Gandhari (C) Kunti (D) Subhadra
5. “Yato dharmas tato jayaḥ” means… (A) Where there is dharma, there is victory (B) Victory at any cost (C) Dharma is difficult (D) War is dharma

Answers: 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 4 A, 5 A

**Life's question from the epics:** Speaking up against wrong. In a court full of elders, only Draupadi asks the question no one wants to answer: had Yudhishthira the right to stake her? (Mahabharata, Sabha Parva, CE 2.60.7–8)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** Before any big decision, pause and consult one elder or trusted friend.

## 09 · September: Bhagavad Gita

**Courage, duty and clear decisions** · 2027-09-19 · Ganesh Chaturthi

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** Do your duty fully and let go of anxiety about the result. A steady mind makes good decisions.

> Tradition holds that Ganesha wrote down the Mahabharata, which contains the Gita, as Vyasa dictated it.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana* | Bhagavad Gita 2.47 | Your right is to action alone, never to its fruits. |
| *Yogasthaḥ kuru karmāṇi … samatvaṁ yoga ucyate* | Bhagavad Gita 2.48 | Act while established in balance; evenness of mind is yoga. |
| *Uddhared ātmanātmānaṁ nātmānam avasādayet* | Bhagavad Gita 6.5 | Lift yourself by your own effort; never pull yourself down. |
| *Yad yad ācarati śreṣṭhas tat tad evetaro janaḥ* | Bhagavad Gita 3.21 | Whatever a great person does, others follow. |

**What to cover**

- Arjuna at Kurukshetra puts down his bow, overwhelmed by doubt; Krishna helps him see clearly, and Arjuna chooses to act.

**Life's question from the epics:** Loyalty versus what is right. Karna turns down Krishna’s offer out of loyalty to Duryodhana, yet admits: where there is dharma, there is victory. (Mahabharata, Udyoga Parva, CE 5.141.33)

## 10 · October: Srimad Bhagavatam

**Devotion, courage and joy in service** · 2027-10-17 · Navaratri · Sharad Purnima

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** Devotion is open to everyone: a five-year-old, a poor friend or a whole village. Love and service, not status, bring us close to the divine.

> We place the Bhagavatam here for the era of its story, Krishna’s life; scholars date the text to about the 9th–10th century CE.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |

**What to cover**

- Dhruva, aged five, does not give up and becomes the Pole Star (Canto 4).
- Prahlada holds to truth against his powerful father (Canto 7).
- Krishna lifts Govardhan, and every villager helps hold it up with a stick (Canto 10).
- Krishna runs barefoot to welcome his poor friend Sudama (Canto 10).

**Circles**

| Circle | Activity |

**Games**

**Quiz**

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)

**Core message:** Around the 6th–5th century BCE, India was full of new cities, trade and bold thinkers. Kingdoms and republics debated and governed through assemblies.

## 12 · December: Gautama Buddha

**The middle way and a compassionate mind** · 2027-12-19 · Annual finale

- **Opening by:** an ambassador or invited delegate (to be announced)
- **Presented by:** volunteer needed

**Core message:** Siddhartha left comfort to understand suffering and found the middle way. His message of a trained mind, right action and compassion is part of India’s shared heritage.

> Scholars place the Buddha’s life around the 5th century BCE; older estimates say the 6th.

**Key verses**

| Verse | Source | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā manoseṭṭhā manomayā* | Dhammapada 1 | Mind comes first; all things are shaped by the mind. |
| *Na hi verena verāni sammantīdha kudācanaṁ, averena ca sammanti esa dhammo sanantano* | Dhammapada 5 | Hatred never ends through hatred, only through love. This is an eternal law. |

**What to cover**

- His life: Lumbini, Bodh Gaya, the first teaching at Sarnath, Kushinagar.
- The Four Noble Truths and the Noble Eightfold Path.
- The Kalama Sutta: test a teaching for yourself before you accept it.
- Our flag’s Dharma Chakra and our national emblem come from Sarnath.

**Games**

**Quiz**

Answers: 1 A, 2 A, 3 A, 4 A, 5 B

**Life's question from the epics:** Second chances. Rama’s advisers doubt Vibhishana, but Rama gives him refuge: shelter for anyone who asks, even Ravana himself. (Ramayana, Yuddha Kanda 18.33)

**Sankalp for 30 days:** Two minutes of loving-kindness each night, and our family’s pledge for 2028 on the wall at home.
